Carousel options deliberately support different amounts of content, since we don't yet know whether each film will carry a blurb.

All five carry the same job: get someone arriving from a LinkedIn advert oriented, and keep the membership CTA visible. They differ in how much of the main site they expose, and how much they behave like a campaign page rather than a website page.

NAV 03

Conversion-first

NewSingle CTA

Logo, one CTA, and a quiet link back to the main site — no navigation at all. The standard pattern for paid-traffic landing pages: every exit that isn't the CTA is removed. A riptide rule under the bar ties it to the campaign without changing the component. Best paired with a sticky bottom CTA on mobile.

NAV 04

Anchored section nav with progress

NewTwo-tierInteractive

Standard navy bar on top, with a white anchor bar beneath that tracks the reader's position through the page and shows a progress line. Aimed squarely at Dal's mobile point — someone arriving from a LinkedIn video lands mid-funnel, and this shows them how much page there is and lets them jump straight to the films. Anchors are in-page, so no one is sent off to the main site mid-journey.

Video carousel

Six treatments for the six campaign films. The important variable is how much copy each film carries — options range from a full blurb per film down to title-only, plus one that needs no carousel at all. Every option meets the brief's four requirements: browse between videos, clearly identify the selected one, support descriptive text where it exists, and work on mobile.

Split stage — dark

Previously shown Title + topic + blurb + link

Large stage with the detail panel alongside, thumbnails beneath. Needs roughly 200–300 characters per film. This is the original brand-guidelines aesthetic, kept here for comparison.

CAROUSEL 03

Wide stage, title only

New Title only

The films carry themselves. One big stage, the title below it, and a row of progress bars instead of thumbnails. Needs nothing but a title per film — and no poster images either, since only the active film is ever shown. The strongest option if copy doesn't materialise, and the least work for CLA Global to populate.

CAROUSEL 04

Six-up grid — no carousel

New Title + short line, or title only

All six films visible at once, each playing in place. Reuses the benefit component's coloured rule, so it reads as an existing pattern rather than a new one. Every film gets equal weight and nothing is hidden behind an interaction — which matters for paid traffic, where people rarely click through a carousel. Blurbs are optional: drop them and the cards still work.

CAROUSEL 05

Playlist beside stage

New Title + topic + duration; blurb below stage

A familiar video-player pattern: the stage on the left, a scrollable playlist on the right showing every film with its duration. All six titles are readable without interacting, unlike a thumbnail rail. The blurb sits under the stage, so it can be long, short or absent without disturbing the layout — the most tolerant option if the copy is still unknown.

CAROUSEL 06

Mobile-first vertical

New Title + duration 390 × device

A swipeable strip of 9:16 slides. Dal's point was that most people will watch the ad on LinkedIn on their phone and then land here — this keeps them in the same vertical format they just came from, rather than making them rotate or squint at a letterboxed 16:9. Assumes vertical cuts of the films exist; if only 16:9 masters are available, this option falls away.

Getting these into Figma

Run Figma export mode from the toolbar at the top, then capture with html.to.design or html2design. Both rebuild the rendered DOM as native Figma layers — frames, text nodes, fills, borders and vectors — rather than flat images. Each block above is already sized to a fixed frame width and labelled with the layer name it should carry. Nothing on this page uses backdrop-filter, clip-path, fixed positioning or layout transforms, all of which import unreliably. Colours are declared as CSS custom properties matching the CLA token names, so they map onto Figma variables cleanly.
